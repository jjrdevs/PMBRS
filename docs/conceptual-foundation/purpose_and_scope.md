## Purpose

PMBRS is a personal behavioral measurement, experimentation, and representation learning platform designed to discover reproducible statistical associations among behaviors, interventions, contexts, and outcomes using multimodal longitudinal data.

Its purpose is to support:

- structured self-experimentation,
- behavioral comparison,
- longitudinal pattern discovery,
- evidence-informed personal interpretation,
- behavioral representation learning research,
- reproducible analysis of personal multimodal behavior over time.

PMBRS is fundamentally:

- a behavioral instrumentation platform,
- a representation learning research platform,
- an experimentation infrastructure system.

It is not intended to function as:

- a clinical system,
- a diagnostic engine,
- a deterministic psychological inference engine,
- an autonomous behavioral optimization system.

## Explicitly Out of Scope

PMBRS is explicitly out of scope for:

- clinical diagnosis,
- deterministic mental-state inference,
- automated causal claims,
- simplistic productivity scoring,
- automated behavioral prescriptions,
- fully autonomous behavioral optimization,
- real-time intervention systems,
- closed-loop behavioral manipulation,
- exhaustive raw surveillance,
- commercial multi-user platform architecture.

PMBRS does not autonomously optimize, manipulate, or intervene in user behavior through closed-loop behavioral control systems.

## Success Criteria

### System Success

PMBRS is successful when it:

- improves longitudinal behavioral evaluation,
- supports useful experimentation,
- increases self-observation quality,
- produces reproducible behavioral comparisons,
- enables consistent temporal and multimodal analysis,
- generates reproducible and statistically supported insights that meaningfully improve the user’s ability to evaluate and refine behaviors and interventions over time.

### Model Success

Models are successful when they demonstrate:

- embedding stability,
- temporal coherence,
- modality robustness,
- clustering stability,
- reproducibility across reruns and reprocessing.

## Optimization Priorities

PMBRS should optimize for:

1. practically useful behavioral insight,
2. scientific rigor,
3. reproducibility,
4. automation of routine operational processes,
5. extensibility,
6. privacy preservation,
7. low-maintenance long-term operation.

Human effort should focus primarily on:

- experiment design,
- model development,
- interpretation,
- architectural governance.

## Operational Philosophy

PMBRS is batch-analysis-first rather than real-time-first.

This affects:

- compute orchestration,
- storage topology,
- synchronization,
- evaluation architecture,
- feature extraction,
- model retraining,
- privacy assumptions.

Real-time systems are downstream applications rather than constitutional infrastructure.
