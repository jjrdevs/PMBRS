## Authority Hierarchy

PMBRS uses a multi-layer authority model separating:

- observational evidence,
- operational structure,
- derived analytical interpretation.

## Authoritative Artifacts

Authoritative artifacts include:

- raw behavioral events,
- wearable telemetry,
- browser/application telemetry,
- journal text,
- intervention declarations,
- calendar/context artifacts,
- timestamps,
- user-authored annotations,
- canonical aligned windows.

These represent irreducible historical evidence.

Authoritative artifacts:

- may not be overwritten,
- must preserve lineage,
- must remain reproducible,
- must remain temporally anchored.

## Canonical Operational Structure

Canonical windows form the operational temporal backbone of PMBRS.

They provide:

- modality synchronization,
- multimodal comparability,
- deterministic temporal alignment,
- reproducible aggregation.

Canonical windows are operational infrastructure rather than semantic interpretation.

## Derived Artifacts

Derived artifacts include:

- feature vectors,
- embeddings,
- clusters,
- inferred events,
- evaluations,
- summaries,
- semantic labels,
- sentiment outputs,
- LLM-generated interpretations.

Derived artifacts are:

- model-dependent,
- probabilistic,
- replaceable,
- reproducible,
- lineage-traceable.

Derived artifacts may never overwrite authoritative artifacts.

## Interpretive Constraints

All inferred outputs must:

- preserve uncertainty,
- expose provenance,
- distinguish association from causation,
- remain subordinate to observational evidence.
